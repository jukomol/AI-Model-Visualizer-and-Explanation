## Generating data by learning to denoise

How do you teach a network to *create* images, audio or molecules? **Generative Adversarial Networks** (Goodfellow et al., 2014) pit a generator against a discriminator. They are powerful, but notoriously unstable to train. **Diffusion models** (Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015; Ho et al., 2020) take a gentler route. They destroy data with noise one small step at a time, then learn to run the process backwards.

## The forward process

Starting from a data point $\mathbf x_0$, add a little Gaussian noise at each of $T$ steps with variances $\beta_1, \dots, \beta_T$. Because a sum of Gaussians is Gaussian, we can jump straight to any step:

$$
q(\mathbf x_t \mid \mathbf x_0) = \mathcal N\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,\mathbf x_0,\ (1 - \bar\alpha_t)\,I\big), \qquad \bar\alpha_t = \prod_{s=1}^{t}(1 - \beta_s)
$$

In words: $\mathbf x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,\mathbf x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\boldsymbol\epsilon$ with $\boldsymbol\epsilon \sim \mathcal N(0, I)$. As $t \to T$, $\bar\alpha_t \to 0$ and the data becomes pure noise.

2. Compare the schedules on the ᾱ chart. The **linear** schedule (Ho et al.) destroys information quickly in the middle steps. The **cosine** schedule (Nichol & Dhariwal, 2021) keeps more signal for longer, so more of the timesteps carry useful signal.

## The reverse process

To generate, start from noise $\mathbf x_T \sim \mathcal N(0, I)$ and repeatedly denoise. The key quantity is the **posterior mean** $\mathbb E[\mathbf x_0 \mid \mathbf x_t]$, the best guess of the clean point given a noisy one. A diffusion model trains a network $\boldsymbol\epsilon_\theta(\mathbf x_t, t)$ to predict the added noise, which is equivalent:

$$
L = \mathbb E_{t,\,\mathbf x_0,\,\boldsymbol\epsilon}\Big\|\boldsymbol\epsilon - \boldsymbol\epsilon_\theta\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\mathbf x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\boldsymbol\epsilon,\ t\big)\Big\|^2
$$

<Callout type="math" title="The optimal denoiser used here">

For a finite training set $\{\mathbf x^{(i)}\}$, the posterior mean has a closed form: a softmax-weighted average of the training points,

$$
\hat{\mathbf x}_0(\mathbf x_t) = \sum_i w_i\,\mathbf x^{(i)}, \qquad w_i \propto \exp\!\Big(-\frac{\|\mathbf x_t - \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,\mathbf x^{(i)}\|^2}{2(1 - \bar\alpha_t)}\Big)
$$

This visualizer uses that *exact* optimum instead of a trained network, so the sampling dynamics are shown without training noise. There is a lesson hidden in it: the perfect denoiser reproduces the training points exactly, so it memorises. Real diffusion models generalise precisely because their networks are smooth, imperfect approximations of it.

</Callout>

**DDIM** sampling (Song, Meng & Ermon, 2020) takes large deterministic steps. From the estimate $\hat{\mathbf x}_0$ it recovers the implied noise $\hat{\boldsymbol\epsilon}$ and jumps to an earlier timestep $t'$:

$$
\mathbf x_{t'} = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_{t'}}\,\hat{\mathbf x}_0 + \sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_{t'}}\,\hat{\boldsymbol\epsilon}
$$

### Things to try (reverse)

1. Switch to **Reverse (sample)** and press **Denoise**. Samples drift from a Gaussian blob onto the data manifold.
2. With **1 sampling step**, every sample collapses towards the *mean* of the data: one giant step can only use the average denoiser.
3. Increase the steps. Samples spread out and land on the moons. Compare how many steps the linear and cosine schedules need to reach the same accuracy.
4. Try the spiral and circles datasets. Thin structures need more steps.

<Callout type="insight" title="From 2-D points to images">

Stable Diffusion and similar models run exactly this loop, but on images, or on compressed "latent" representations of them. A U-Net or Transformer is conditioned on the timestep and a text embedding, and techniques such as classifier-free guidance steer samples towards the prompt.

</Callout>

## Summary

- The forward process mixes data with Gaussian noise, $\mathbf x_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\mathbf x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\boldsymbol\epsilon$, with a chosen schedule.
- Generation runs the process backwards using an estimate of $\mathbb E[\mathbf x_0 \mid \mathbf x_t]$, learned in practice by predicting the noise.
- Deterministic samplers such as DDIM trade the number of steps against accuracy.
