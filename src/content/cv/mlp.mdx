## Bending the decision boundary

Logistic regression draws one straight line. A **multilayer perceptron** first maps the input through one or more **hidden layers** of non-linear units, and then draws a line in that new representation. Each hidden unit contributes one "bend", and together they can wrap around spirals, rings and XOR.

$$
f(\mathbf x) = W_2\,\phi(W_1\mathbf x + \mathbf b_1) + \mathbf b_2
$$

## The universal approximation theorem

<Callout type="math" title="Cybenko (1989), Hornik, Stinchcombe & White (1989)">

A network with a **single** hidden layer of sigmoid units can approximate any continuous function on a compact domain to any accuracy, given enough hidden units. The same holds for ReLU and most non-polynomial activations.

</Callout>

The theorem says that such a network *exists*. It says nothing about how many units you need, whether gradient descent will find the right weights, or whether the result will generalise. In practice **depth** is far more efficient than width. Deep networks build features hierarchically and represent some functions with exponentially fewer units than shallow ones.

### Things to try

1. Train with the defaults (2 hidden layers × 16 tanh units). Watch the regions wrap around the spiral arms.
2. Drop to **1 hidden layer with 4 units**. There are too few bends, and the boundary cannot follow the spiral: that is **under-fitting**.
3. Go to **4 layers × 32 units**, add more noise, and train for a long time. The boundary starts carving islands around individual points: that is **over-fitting**.
4. Switch the activation to **ReLU**. The boundary becomes piecewise linear, polygonal rather than smooth.
5. Try **sigmoid** with 4 layers. Training is noticeably slower because of saturation (see *Activation functions*).
6. Paint your own dataset. A ring of one class around another is a classic test.

## Multi-class outputs: softmax

For $K$ classes, the network outputs $K$ scores (logits) $z_k$, which the softmax turns into probabilities:

$$
p_k = \frac{e^{z_k}}{\sum_j e^{z_j}}, \qquad L = -\log p_y, \qquad \frac{\partial L}{\partial z_k} = p_k - [k = y]
$$

Paint four classes with the brush selector (or pick the *Four clusters* preset) to see a softmax classifier carve the plane into four regions.

<Callout type="insight" title="Depth versus width in practice">

For small tabular problems, 1–3 hidden layers of 32–256 ReLU units, trained with Adam, is a strong start. When you need more capacity, grow depth together with good initialisation, normalisation and residual connections, not width alone.

</Callout>

## Summary

- Hidden layers with non-linear activations let a network represent curved decision boundaries.
- One hidden layer is enough *in principle* (universal approximation), but depth is usually more efficient.
- Too little capacity under-fits and too much over-fits, so watch training and validation performance.
